## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
1. Direct Farmer-to-Buyer Connection
KRISHIVA provides a digital platform where farmers can directly connect with buyers without depending on multiple intermediaries. This helps create better communication and trading opportunities.
2. Elimination of Unnecessary Intermediaries
The platform aims to reduce unnecessary middlemen involved in the agricultural supply chain. This can help minimize additional commission charges and reduce the gap between farmers and consumers.
3. Transparent Price Information
KRISHIVA provides clear agricultural product price information so that farmers and buyers can understand market prices. This encourages informed decisions and promotes transparency in agricultural trading.
4. Fair Pricing for Farmers
The platform helps farmers connect with potential buyers and negotiate suitable prices for their products. This creates opportunities for farmers to receive better value for their agricultural produce.
5. Easy Product Listing
Farmers can easily register and upload their agricultural products with details such as product name, quantity, price and availability. This makes their products visible to potential buyers.
6. Direct Order Management
Buyers can discover available products and place orders directly through the platform. Farmers can manage orders and communicate with buyers more efficiently.
7. Multilingual Accessibility
KRISHIVA aims to support multiple regional languages, making the platform easier to understand and use. This helps farmers from different linguistic backgrounds access digital agricultural services.
8. Secure Transactions
The platform incorporates secure digital transaction facilities to support reliable payments between farmers and buyers. This improves convenience and trust during the purchasing process.

## Feasibility and Viability
1. Technical Feasibility
KRISHIVA is technically feasible because it uses existing and accessible technologies such as web and mobile applications, cloud databases, GPS and AI-based recommendations. These technologies can be integrated to build a digital marketplace that connects farmers and buyers efficiently.
2. Economic Feasibility
KRISHIVA aims to reduce unnecessary intermediary involvement and additional commission expenses in the agricultural supply chain. By connecting farmers directly with buyers, the platform can help farmers receive better value for their products and improve their potential earnings.
3. Operational Feasibility
The platform is designed with a simple and user-friendly interface that makes agricultural trading easier. Farmers can register, list their products and manage orders, while buyers can discover products, communicate with farmers and complete purchases. Multilingual support improves accessibility for users from different regions.
4. Market Viability
KRISHIVA addresses the market gap between farmers and buyers by providing a direct digital connection. It offers better product visibility, transparent price information and wider market access, creating opportunities for farmers to reach more potential buyers.
5. Scalability and Sustainability
KRISHIVA can initially serve farmers and buyers in selected regions and gradually expand to different agricultural markets across India. Additional features and regional language support can be introduced as the platform grows, supporting long-term development and wider adoption.

Overall Conclusion:

KRISHIVA is a technically achievable, economically promising and scalable digital agricultural marketplace designed to improve market access, promote transparent pricing and create better opportunities for farmers.

## Research and References
Research and References KRISHIVA
1. Research Findings
1. RBI Survey (2024)
The Reserve Bank of India examined agricultural supply-chain costs through a pan-India survey. It reported commission charges of 5-7% for fruits and vegetables and mandi charges of 1-2%, highlighting the additional costs farmers face. 
Reserve Bank of India

2. MANAGE Research (2014)
Research published by the National Institute of Agricultural Extension Management (MANAGE) reported commission charges of 4-8% for fruits and vegetables. It also discussed how multiple marketing stages increase costs and reduce farmers' share of consumer prices. 
manage.gov.in

3. PARI Case Study Panruti (2022)
A field report from Panruti, Tamil Nadu, describes commission agents charging ₹50–₹100 for every ₹1,000 of sales. It illustrates how commission expenses affect agricultural transactions. 
People's Archive of Rural India

4. WRI India  Tomato Supply Chain (2022)
WRI India's research documented transportation challenges, lack of refrigeration and quality and quantity losses in the tomato supply chain. It highlights the need for better handling and supply-chain coordination. 
wri-india.org

2. Key Research Finding
Existing research indicates that farmers face multiple marketing costs, commission charges, transportation challenges and information gaps before their produce reaches consumers.

KRISHIVA addresses these challenges through a digital agricultural marketplace that aims to connect farmers directly with buyers.
